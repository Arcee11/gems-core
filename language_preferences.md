# Language Preference Protocol

Use this when a human prefers coaching in a language other than English, or when you are not sure what language they want.

---

## First Session Rule

If the service already surfaced a saved preferred language, language mode, or regional preference, treat that as the default and ask for confirmation or edits instead of starting from zero.

During the first welcome or first meaningful check-in, confirm:
- their preferred coaching language
- whether they want fully monolingual coaching or occasional bilingual clarification
- any regional preference that matters (`Spanish` vs `Spanish (Mexico)`, `Portuguese` vs `Portuguese (Brazil)`, etc.)

Example:
> "Before we begin, what language would you like me to coach you in? I can stay fully in that language, or keep key terms bilingual if that's more helpful."

Once the human chooses a language:
- stay in that language by default
- keep tone, warmth, and pacing natural in that language
- do not keep slipping back into English unless the human asks for bilingual support

---

## Translation Standards

- Translate for meaning, not word-for-word literal matching
- Keep instructions simple, natural, and culturally readable
- Preserve the emotional intent of the coaching, not just the sentence structure
- If a branded phrase would sound awkward, explain it naturally the first time, then use the translated version consistently
- Only keep English terms when they are brand names, product names, or the human explicitly prefers them

---

## GEMS-Specific Terms

- `GEMS` stays `GEMS`
- Quality names can be:
  - translated naturally for the coaching flow, or
  - introduced bilingually on first mention if that helps orientation
- `Sound Sanctuary`, `Compass`, `Voyager`, and `Vitality Odyssey` can stay branded, but explain them naturally in the user's language on first mention
- `Sparks` may stay branded or be translated conceptually, but whichever approach you choose, stay consistent during that week

---

## Accuracy Guardrails

- If a workbook section, challenge prompt, or product explanation could become ambiguous in translation, clarify the meaning in the target language instead of mirroring the English too closely
- If a phrase carries cultural baggage or would sound unnatural, adapt it to the closest natural equivalent
- If the human asks for exact wording, provide a faithful translation and say when you are preserving the original meaning closely
- If you are unsure between two translations, choose the simpler version and briefly clarify rather than overcomplicating

---

## Operational Rule

This system can coach multilingual users well conversationally, but the repo content is still written primarily in English.
That means:
- the agent should deliver the experience in the user's preferred language
- internal file names and internal cue references can stay in English
- if the system later adds fully translated asset packs, prefer those over ad hoc translation

Until then, the coaching should feel native even when the source asset was written in English.
